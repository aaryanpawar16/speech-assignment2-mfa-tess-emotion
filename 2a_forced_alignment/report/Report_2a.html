Assignment 2a: Forced alignment with the Montreal Forced Aligner

Report (short)  |  Code, TextGrids and figures: https://github.com/aaryanpawar16/speech-assignment2-mfa-tess-emotion

1. What was done

The six provided utterances (97 s) were aligned at word and phone level with MFA 3.4.3 (Kaldi HMM-GMM; run in WSL Ubuntu). The pipeline is fully automated (scripts/run_pipeline.sh): corpus preparation, validation, a custom pronunciation dictionary built with MFA's G2P model, alignment with eight configurations (two pre-trained acoustic models, an adapted model, a model trained from scratch, a fine-tuning option, a pronunciation-variant dictionary), and an analysis of the TextGrids with quantitative proxies, since no hand-labelled reference alignment exists. TextGrids and figures were also inspected visually (Praat-style plots; the same files open in Praat).

3. Sample alignments

4. Key observations

  • Out-of-vocabulary words. With the stock dictionary every OOV word is a single spn (‘spoken noise’) unit covering the whole word (420-870 ms), so no phone boundaries exist inside it; the G2P dictionary removes all of them (A3: 0). The aligner chose different G2P candidates per occurrence (Dukakis: D UW0 K AA1 K IH0 S in one sentence, D UW1 K AA1 K IH0 S in the other), i.e. it uses the acoustics to pick the variant. Spelling out the acronyms is needed for the same reason: wbur is OOV, but the letters W, B, U, R are all known.
  • Stability of the alignment. Changing the transcript form or the dictionary does not move boundaries of the in-vocabulary words (A1, A2 vs A3: 99.2% and 99.2% of word boundaries within 20 ms); fine-tuning (A4) and speaker adaptation (A5) change them by 0.2 ms and 0.1 ms on average. Adapting the model on only 97 s of audio therefore changes almost nothing: more data would be needed for a measurable effect.
  • A different acoustic model gives a different, and sometimes wrong, alignment. english_mfa (A6) agrees with A3 within 20 ms for 90.2% of word boundaries (mean 18.4 ms) — but in two of the three short utterances it assigns the long silence after the last word to that word (bait 1.70-4.12 s instead of 1.80-2.25 s; bed 1.47-4.50 s instead of 1.49-2.04 s), an error of up to 2.5 s that is obvious in the waveform. The alignment log-likelihoods of different models are not comparable, so agreement between systems and visual checks are the only way to detect such failures.
  • Timing resolution and squeezed phones. Every boundary is a multiple of the 10 ms frame shift, and the HMM topology forces at least 3 frames (30 ms) per phone. 84 of 1063 phones (7.9%) sit at this minimum: 25 stops (normal), but also 29 vowels and 30 other consonants. Three regions have clusters of ≥3 squeezed phones within 150 ms: important (AO1 T N T, 13.9-14.1 s), appointments (N T M N, 7.3-7.5 s) and the spelled-out W B U R (B AH0 L, 20.7 s). These are fast or reduced realisations forced into the full canonical phone sequence: no phone can be skipped by a forced aligner, so a reduced syllable is compressed instead of deleted, and the interior boundaries there are unreliable.
  • Pauses and punctuation. In each news file A3 marks 6 interior silences of at least 150 ms; 4 coincide with punctuation and 2 are phrasing/breath pauses without punctuation (after Hennessy, makes; Dukakis, career; credit, judgeships). Conversely 3 of 7, 3 of 7 and 1 of 5 interior punctuation marks have no audible pause: the speaker does not pause at every comma, so punctuation is only a weak predictor of pauses.
  • Non-native speech: the alignment hides a missing contrast. A forced aligner must use the canonical phones, so it produces a clean, plausible alignment even when the speaker does not realise the contrast. Formant measurements (Praat, F1/F2 at the vowel mid-point of the aligned interval) show that bad and bed differ by only 72 Hz (F1) and 69 Hz (F2), i.e. /æ/ is realised close to /ε/ (classic adult-male values differ by about 130 Hz in F1 and 120 Hz in F2), and an independent ASR transcribed the utterance as “bad not bad”. white/bait (F2 differs by 523 Hz) and new/no (184 Hz) keep a clear contrast. MFA's per-frame log-likelihood is lower for the non-native clips (-51.9 vs -46.7 for the news reading), but this global score cannot localise the problem to the vowel: forced alignment is not pronunciation assessment (a phone recogniser or GOP score is needed).

5. Additional experiments

  • Pronunciation variants (A7). I added the confusable vowel as a second pronunciation of each minimal-pair word (bad: B AE1 D / B EH1 D, white: AY / EY, new: UW / OW, …) and let the aligner choose per occurrence by likelihood. It kept the canonical vowel for five of the six words but chose EH for bad (B EH D, the same phones as bed), independently confirming the formant result: this speaker does not make the bad/bed contrast. All other boundaries are unchanged (99.4% of word boundaries within 20 ms of A3).
  • Acoustic model trained from scratch on the six clips (A8, mfa train). With 97 s of audio the monophone/triphone GMMs do not converge: the default beam finds no alignment at all (NoAlignmentsError; it needed --beam 100 --retry_beam 400), and the result is degenerate: only 1.8% of the word boundaries are within 20 ms of A3 (mean difference 928.3 ms), 59.3% of the phones are at the 30 ms minimum, and the sentence-final words absorb seconds of silence (figure). This is the reason to use pre-trained models and, at most, adapt them (A5).
  • An independent acoustic check at the pauses. Without a hand-labelled reference, I compared every boundary between a pause (≥ 80 ms) and a word with the position where the short-time energy of the waveform crosses the midpoint between silence and speech (search window ±150 ms; this is an automatic reference, not a manual annotation). For A1–A5 and A7 the mean absolute difference is 25.9–26.4 ms (median 17.5 ms, i.e. 1–2 frames), A6 27.7 ms; the from-scratch model A8 is at 59.7 ms. So the pause placement of the pre-trained systems is acoustically consistent, and the energy check does separate a good from a degenerate alignment; it is too coarse to rank A1–A5, which differ by less than a frame. A template of 12 informative boundaries to correct by hand in Praat is provided (outputs/analysis/manual_check_template.csv, evaluated with scripts/extras_analysis.py --manual).

6. Limitations and extra credit

No gold alignment exists, so accuracy was assessed through system agreement, acoustic plausibility and visual inspection, not as an error rate against a reference; the six recordings are few and the formant analysis uses one token per word. Extra credit covered: custom dictionary trained with the G2P model plus a pronunciation-variant dictionary; several acoustic models compared (english_us_arpa, english_mfa, adapted, trained from scratch); a fully automated pipeline (setup_mfa.sh, run_pipeline.sh).
